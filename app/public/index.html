The problem

Rich in grain, short on cash.

A producer can have tens of thousands of dollars stored in a silo bag and still lack the working capital to start the next season. The farming calendar doesn't wait for the sale.

Slow, bilateral credit

Credit against a warrant goes through a bank or a broker today: paperwork, manual approval and timelines that don't match the season.

Selling at the worst moment

The alternative is selling right after the harvest, when supply peaks and prices are at their lowest.

An asset that's already law

The warrant is regulated by Law 9,643, modernised by Decree 70/2023: electronic documents and divisible lots. Nothing new to invent.

How it works

From silo bag to USDC in four steps.

The certifier registers the grain, the contract holds the collateral, the pool lends. No intermediaries in between.

01

Issue the warrant

The storage facility certifies the deposit and mints the digital warrant — one token per ton, straight to your wallet.

02

Lock it as collateral

Deposit the warrant in the on-chain vault. The program holds it — nobody can move it, not even us.

03

Receive USDC instantly

The open pool funds your loan in the same transaction — up to 70% of the grain's value.

04

Repay, get it back

Repay anytime and the warrant returns automatically. If the price collapses, liquidation keeps the pool whole.

Legal framework

The warrant is already law. Arva makes it liquid.

Arva doesn't need a new legal figure. It builds on a 111-year-old instrument that recent reforms opened up to electronic platforms.

Law 9,643 · 1914

The original instrument

Creates warrants and deposit certificates over agricultural products. Still in force.

Decree 70/2023

The modernisation

Removes restrictions and widens the products and parties covered by the regime.

Decree 640/2024

Electronic issuance

Warrants can be issued and traded electronically — on issuer or third-party platforms, public or private.

Arva is a prototype and holds no registration. In production, warrants would be issued by a warehouse company listed in the official registry.

FAQ

What everyone asks.

Is this real money?+
No. Arva runs on Solana devnet, the test network. The USDC is test money with no value, but every action is a real transaction you can verify on the explorer.
Is the contract audited?+
Not yet. It's a hackathon prototype and we say so up front. An audit comes before any real money.
Who issues the warrants?+
In the demo, a simulated certifier run by the team. In production, a warehouse company registered under Law 9,643 would issue them.
Where does the grain price come from?+
From a simulated oracle the team updates to show liquidations. Production would use an independent price feed.
How do I try it?+
Install Phantom, switch it to devnet and open the app. The built-in guide walks you through every screen.
